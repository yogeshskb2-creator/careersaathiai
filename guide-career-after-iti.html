Career options after ITI: apprenticeship, jobs and your own work

Last reviewed: 22 October 2026 हिंदी में पढ़ें

Finished ITI and not sure what next? Here are the main paths — apprenticeship, a job, or your own work — and how to start each one safely.

An ITI certificate gives you a practical trade skill. Government and private ITIs run trade courses under the Craftsmen Training Scheme of the Directorate General of Training (DGT). According to DGT, there are courses in 169 trades, lasting about 6 months to 2 years. That skill can lead to several paths.

1. Paid apprenticeship (learn while you earn)

An apprenticeship is training inside a real company or government organisation. You learn on the job and receive a stipend. The National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme (NAPS) encourages companies to take apprentices, with government support towards part of the stipend.

  • Register as a candidate on the official national apprenticeship portal and apply to openings in your trade.
  • You must have the minimum qualification set for that trade.
  • An apprenticeship is often the easiest way to get your first real work experience.

2. A job in your trade

Electricians, welders, plumbers, fitters and repair technicians are needed in factories, construction, workshops and service centres. To improve your chances:

  • Keep proof of your skill: your ITI certificate, apprenticeship certificate and photos of work you have done.
  • Some work, such as certain electrical work, may need a licence. Ask your local authority.
  • Search for openings on the National Career Service (NCS) portal.

3. Government recruitment

Many ITI holders look at railway, defence and other government recruitment. The exact qualification, age limit and physical standards are different for every notification.

  • Railways: the Railway Recruitment Boards publish recruitment for posts such as Level-1 and technician on their application portal.
  • Defence: the Indian Army publishes Agniveer and other entries on its official website.
  • Always read the full official notification before applying. Never pay anyone to "set" a government job.

4. Your own work

A repair shop, wiring work or fabrication can become your own business.

  • PM Vishwakarma supports artisans in 18 traditional trades (for example carpenter, blacksmith, mason and tailor) with training, a toolkit e-voucher and collateral-free credit. Registration is done at a Common Service Centre.
  • PM MUDRA Yojana gives collateral-free loans to micro enterprises through banks and other lenders.

Next steps

  1. Add basic computer skills — a course like CCC helps in almost every job today.
  2. Prepare a one-page resume with your trade, skills and any work done.
  3. Choose one path for the next 30 days and follow it.
